# Labyrinth of Triquetra
## A 12-Page Shonen Mathematics Manga Script

**Cast (and no other speaking characters)**
- **Ren** — energetic swordsman; acts only after Kaito notices and Aoi explains.
- **Aoi** — scholar wizard; states formal criteria and gives the reasoning.
- **Kaito** — observant thief; notices visual patterns before each deduction.

**Visual rule for the artist:** Every geometric figure visible in a panel is one of the numbered figures specified below that panel. A later appearance says “reuse Figure X exactly”; this means the same labels, measurements, marks, correspondence, and orientation apply, and no unlisted triangle is to be drawn. Decorative architecture may use triquetra curves and non-triangular polygonal fragments, but must not contain additional triangle figures.

**Notation rule:** `≅` means congruent (same shape and same size). `~` means similar (same shape, proportional size). Corresponding vertex order is always preserved.

- Orientation: `△ABC` points up; `△DEF` points down after a 180° rotation plus reflection. Orientation does not change length or angle.
- Triangle inequality: each triangle has `6+6>6` for all three pairings.
- Proportions: `AB/DE=BC/EF=CA/FD=6/6=1`.

## Why SSA and AAA fail as congruence tests
- **SSA:** Knowing two sides and a non-included angle can lead to the ambiguous case: depending on where the third vertex meets an arc, zero, one, or two non-congruent triangles may satisfy the data. It is therefore not a general congruence criterion.
- **AAA:** Three angles determine shape but not size. Multiplying all sides by any positive scale factor preserves all three angles, so differently sized triangles satisfy the same AAA data. AAA proves similarity, not congruence.
